Beaver MK2 Head

  • Machine Tools

My 1921 Denbig horizontal mill is a great machine. Compared to a turret mill the same size, it's way more rigid. I can take huge cuts without chatter with 1" diameter endmills. I can take cuts so deep cast iron peels off with actual chips, instead of just dust. The major downside to the machine is the vertical head that I have for it does not have a quill feed. it makes repeated drilling operations awkward, and has limited clearance to the table.

I had been looking for milling heads from old Bridgeport machines with a plan to transplant it onto this Denbig, but nothing suitable or close enough to me came up. One day on Ebay, I found a used machinery dealer in Ontario that had this Beaver MK2 head sitting on a pallet, for very reasonable money. It provides a power down feed, 4 speeds, and designed to be easily maintained.

It arrived in rough shape. It needed a lot of cleaning, repacking bearings, inspection, and repair. The spindle nose lock ring was cracked, the back gear selector detent had worn a track in casting, and the spindle bearings needed replacement. I machined a new spindle nose lock ring out of a piece of durabar cast iron, which closely matched the original lock ring. The detent took a bit of careful tig welding, but you can't tell the wear was there. To prevent it wearing in the future, I made a stainless steel plate that has the two detent holes in it as a much harder wear surface. The driven pulley was in bad shape as well, with several large dings and dents in it. It was causing a huge clattering noise that could be heard over hearing protection. I carefully bent the dents back into place, built up more surface with the tig welder, and re-machined the pulley grooves.

I eventually put a variable speed 3 phase motor on it, so I don't change gears often, but to finish off the aesthetics I re-etched a new spindle speed chart based off of the very faded original. I also replaced the missing quill handle with a large wheel welded out of two half circles I bent with a pipe bender. I much prefer it for maintaining feel when using small drills.

To mount the head to the Denbig, I decided to use the overarm bar. I shortened the overarm bar enough to fit on my lathe. Then I cut a fine thread at one end, and a taper at the other with a 1-13 threaded hole. Then I took the large slug of steel that the head came mounted to, and bored a tapered hole in it to fit the taper on the end of the overarm. A large bolt pulls the taper home tightly. The steel bar also has two 5/8" hols in it.

The head mounts to the Denbig on the end of the overarm bar, then a lock ring was made to pull the overarm bar rearwards, tightening the mounting bar against the column of the mill. Then 2 5/8" holes were drilled and tapped using the holes in the mounting bar. The two 5/8" bolts and the overarm bar lock ring create a very rigid 3 point clamping setup.

The head was then completely stripped down to it's component pieces, all bearings were changed, everything was properly lubricated, and reassembled.

Using some guidance from professional spindle rebuilders, I used my lathe's toolpost grinder setup on the mill table and reground the taper on the spindle after installing new bearings, achieving a total indicated run out of less than 0.0002".
