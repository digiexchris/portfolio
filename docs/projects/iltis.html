Iltis

For the love of Made In Canada military hardware

  • Maintenance

This was my 1983 Bombardier Iltis. They were used by the Canadian Forces from the early 70's until they were phased out during the Afghanistan conflict. It was licensed by Bombardier from Volkswagen, who had just started producing them from the German military, along with a 12v civilian version. The 4x4 system was entirely contained within the transmission, and was the precursor to the Audi Quattro. They proved it by winning the 1979 Paris to Dakar rally using a Volkswagen Iltis.

I was working at Wes-T-Rans, and across the street at a used car dealer sat 4 of these, in running and safetied condition. I excitedly paid my $4000 and brought it home.

It has several interesting things to point out:

the suspension was leaf suspension, but the leaf ran width-wise from one steering knuckle, to a center mount in the middle of the frame, and to the other steering knuckle.
The ignition was completely waterproof and radio frequency immune
The CV joints could be removed without the wheel bearings falling apart. The hub had a retaining shaft running through the hub that the stub axle slid into, but the stub axle wasn't responsible for keeping the bearing together, like it is in most modern cars. That meant that with the diff locker and 4x4, you could break up to 6 cv joints before it could not move under it's own power anymore.
The oil pump drive shaft drives the distributor, so if the pump becomes jammed, the engine stops without damage from oil starvation
The frame is made from box section, and completely enclosed and air tight. It's incredibly stiff, and immune to rusting from the inside out. It's rumoured that the frame is filled with Argon.

The surplus parts market dried up quickly. I ended up having to convert the spark plug wires to modern wires, boots, and plugs. I became proficient at matching Volkswagen and Audi consumables like clutches and spark plugs that fit as well as learning military designations for lubricants and cross referencing with other military vehicles.
